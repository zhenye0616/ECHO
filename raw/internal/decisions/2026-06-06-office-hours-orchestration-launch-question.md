## The question was already answered on 2026-06-01 — and the answer was being ignored

Five days prior, the founder agreed to four premises: (1) **the wedge is the orchestration loop, not the bare context layer** (loop = aspirin, validated n=1 active pain, 81 items / 220 review rounds; layer = vitamin); (2) the only validated demand is orchestrator-shaped; (3) the binding constraint is demand-targeting; (4) **"shipping context-layer-only is backwards — it launches the half with no demand and shelves the half with demand."**

Today's framing ("keep orchestration to myself, roll out context-only — already doing this") is a continuation of the strategy that session ruled backwards, re-asked as an open question. Named in-session: a "launch decision" is the inward, fully-controllable move the 06-01 pattern note warned about; the outward gate (the five-orchestrator hunt) was the assignment.

## Hunt status (new evidence since 06-01)

The founder **did run the hunt** — credit on the record: ~7 outbound contacts. Results:

- **1 onboarded** — onto the **context layer only**. First unprompted external value report in company history: *"useful when his computer crashes and he needs to resume; still discovering use cases naturally."*
- **4 ghosted** — but every contact was pitched the **vitamin half**; the loop was never shown to anyone. The ghosts are therefore **unreadable**: pain-not-real and pitch-described-the-wrong-half are indistinguishable. The 06-01 doc predicted this exact confound ("the bare layer captures into a void").
- **2 in pipeline** — not yet pitched.
- **Open (founder did not answer in-session):** whether the onboarded one passes the 🔒 duct-tape orchestrator screen or is the concierge coworker; what the DM sentence that got the yes was, verbatim; unprompted usage frequency since install.

## Reading the crash-resume signal

"Useful when my computer crashes" = the context layer becoming an aspirin at exactly one moment: **discontinuity** (crash, compaction, `/clear`, machine switch, Monday-morning return). This is the burned-insurance finding resurfacing: the layer is *insurance*, valued at loss events ⇒ usage will be **spiky, not daily**. Honest weight: real value report, NOT panic-if-gone; "still discovering use cases" is either exploration or polite indifference. **Discriminator: unprompted retrieval frequency between crashes** — watch, don't ask.

Side yield — a user-discovered pitch sentence: *"your agents never lose state — crash, clear, compact, switch machines, it's all still there."*

## Verdict

**The binary (hoard vs. launch) is false; both horns skip the same gate.**

- **"Keep it to myself" is a non-asset, decaying.** A workflow hoarded by a solo dev with no users is inventory, not moat — nobody is copying what nobody knows exists. Meanwhile the loop's components are being commoditized by the vendors themselves (agent teams, workflows, subagents are shipping natively in Claude Code / Codex now); in ~12 months the multi-agent review loop is table stakes. What vendors structurally cannot ship is the cross-vendor substrate (the founder's own 06-01 Q6 mechanism). The loop's value **as a secret** → 0 on someone else's schedule; its value **as a demand probe** is at peak now.
- **"Launch it" is a packaging decision without a *who*.** Premise 3 (demand-targeting) is still the binding constraint. Launching to nobody-in-particular yields the polite-"cool" false positive the 06-01 doc warned about.

**Resolution: the loop is the DEMO; the layer is the INSTALL.** Don't package, don't launch, don't hide — *show*:

1. For the 2 pipeline prospects: lead with a live 20-minute loop demo (spec → strategist → builder worktrees → cross-vendor review rounds → merge, with the substrate visibly carrying context between vendors). The sentence: "I ship as one person with 220 cross-vendor review rounds behind me."
2. **Watch their hands, not their words.** Grab at the loop ("how do I get that") ⇒ wedge confirmed, V1 spec detonates as the 06-01 doc anticipated, packaging the loop becomes the roadmap conversation. Grab at the layer ("it remembers everything across my tools?") ⇒ context-first rollout validated by a human grabbing it rather than by default.
3. Show the loop to the already-onboarded user too — warmest asset, has seen only half the product.

Either grab-direction wins. The only losing move is the current one: deciding launch strategy for a product no prospect has ever seen, based on ghosts who were shown something else.
