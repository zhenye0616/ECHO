## Q4 — Narrowest wedge: **"ECHO writes the recap"**

The **daily** cross-tool org recap — Slack + Granola + eng capture — of *what happened, what was decided (confirm/dismiss cards embedded), what's still open, and where claims diverge from reality* — with the fire-day recap as hero mode and June 24 as the demo anchor.

**Disqualified alternatives (kept on roadmap, not pilot):**
- **Live decision surfacing during fires** — blind to the side channels that carried June 24's real plan; unfalsifiable until the next emergency schedules itself; humans ignore bots mid-fire; hardest build before the Jul 18 freeze. V2 hero feature.
- **Standing ledger as the offer** — disqualified by the founder's own launch-day behavior: three valid decision cards posted to his Slack that morning, **zero confirms, even from the builder**. Nobody buys an empty filing cabinet; a queryable-only ledger reproduces Granola's pull problem (the founder's own critique, aimed back). The ledger **emerges as a byproduct** of confirming cards inside a recap the buyer already reads.

**Why this survives the competitive frame (same-day scan):** Granola is meetings-bound by structure and identity ($125M "context layer" pivot notwithstanding; realistic subsume window on the *meetings slice* ~2–4 quarters). Slack AI recaps are single-tool chronology. The recap's moat is the **diagnosis layer** — misalignment detection, decisions-to-confirm, readiness-drift — which Q5's evidence says is what the buyer actually valued. Interop agents (MCP-style pull-joins) answer one question once and leave no ratified record: **the query is commodity; the record is the product.**

**The offer (to pressure-test on P. verbatim):** *"You asked me to hand-build the June 24 recap. ECHO now writes it every day — across Slack, meetings, and my eng work, ending with the decisions to confirm. We run it 30 days; if you stop reading it, we kill it. $X/mo after that."* Anchor $X against the $3–6k/month duct-tape; even $100/mo on the company card converts enthusiasm into evidence. Opener that makes it undeniable: **"Your three pieces of June 25 feedback — where are they written down?"**

**Success / kill:** unprompted consumption past week 1; P. cites or queries the record at least once without being reminded; the daily meeting's "what happened" half measurably shrinks. Kill: 30 days of recaps with zero unprompted interaction.

## Q6 — Future-fit: mechanism, not weather

**The unified cross-tool context exists only in the operator's head, and the operator is a lossy message bus.** Per-tool vendor memory *widens* the gap: each vendor compounds inside its wall (structurally forbidden from the join — their business depends on it) while the join, where decisions actually live, belongs to nobody. Sharpened one notch: in the agent-dense future, **the human isn't even on the bus** — agent-to-agent work produces decision-grade context no human witnessed once, so the unified context won't exist *anywhere*, not even in a head. ECHO's role grows from "relieve the human bus" to "the only place org context exists at all." With ECHO, the human stops being the message bus and becomes the decision-maker. Hostile-question answer on record: pull-time interop joins re-derive and forget; no accumulation, no confirmation, no supersession.
