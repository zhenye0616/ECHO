## Why the multi-hat opening is the right one (strategic note, not pitch copy)

The 06-01 office-hours named the binding constraint: demand-targeting — we couldn't name person #2. The multi-hat cofounder fixes that:

- **It's a nameable ICP:** a technical cofounder at a 2–5 person AI-native startup. The founder *is* one; the n=2 CEO loop counterpart is the adjacent buyer.
- **It collapses the two-buyer problem.** The layered story otherwise has an individual buyer (engineer, personal context) and an org buyer (team alignment) — two sales motions. In a small startup they are the same human on the same day. The person who feels "my AI tools keep forgetting me" at 10am feels "I just spent an hour re-explaining eng priorities to my cofounder" at 2pm.
- **It makes the layers felt, not architectural.** A cofounder wearing eng + PM + support hats already context-switches across the exact boundaries ECHO's layers formalize. The pitch doesn't have to explain what a "context boundary" is — the listener lives it.

## The narrative arc (five beats)

### Beat 1 — Open: the many-hats reality

> "At a three-person startup, the title on your deck is 'cofounder' but the job is five jobs. You're the engineer in Cursor and Claude Code all morning, the PM in Linear after lunch, the account manager in Slack with your first customer at 4, and the strategist in a Granola-recorded call at 5. Every one of those tools has an AI in it now — and every single one of them meets you as a stranger. You spend a meaningful slice of every day re-introducing yourself to your own tools, and re-explaining one hat's decisions to another hat's tools."

The pain is stated as *one person's* pain. No org chart needed.

### Beat 2 — The personal wedge: your context layer

> "ECHO fixes that for you first. It's a personal context layer that runs on your machine: it captures what your tools already see — your coding agents, your terminal, your git history — and serves it back to every AI surface you touch, through MCP. Raw context never leaves your machine. Day one, alone, no team setup: every AI you use stops being a stranger."

This is the per-seat, bottom-up land. It matches the shipped reality (node at n=1, echoctl tarball, machine-scoped capture) and the V1 cohort (indie AI builders).

### Beat 3 — The first promotion: cofounder alignment

> "Then your cofounder asks the question every cofounder asks: 'why is this the priority?' Today you answer it in a meeting, again in Slack, and a third time when it's forgotten. With ECHO, the decision-grade moments in your work — the *why* behind what you built — get proposed to you, you confirm with one click, and they land in a shared team store. Your cofounder asks ECHO in Slack and gets your actual rationale, in context, without pulling you out of flow. Nothing you didn't approve ever leaves your machine."

This is the propose-confirm gate as the emotional center of the pitch: it is simultaneously the privacy story and the alignment story. Grounded in the n=2 CEO loop and the 107/108/109 intake work.

### Beat 4 — The layered growth story: ECHO grows the way your company grows

> "Here's the part that matters as you grow. Those hats you're wearing? Each one becomes a person, then a team, then a department. ECHO's structure grows along the exact same lines: every team is an atomic context store — a hub for its members, and a single node to the level above it. The same rule applies at every boundary: raw context stays where it's produced; only human-approved, decision-grade context flows up. Engineering's day-to-day noise stays in engineering; the *decisions* reach product; product's decisions reach the exec level. No re-platforming at 20 people, no 'enterprise edition' at 200 — the same primitive, composed."

This is the fractal thesis rendered as a growth promise instead of an architecture diagram. The multi-hat opening pays off here: the layers aren't introduced as new machinery, they're what the listener's hats become when the company grows.

### Beat 5 — Close: the one-liner

> "ECHO is your org's context layer — raw context stays where it's produced, decision-grade context flows to where it's needed. We don't make AI smarter. We make every AI smarter about you — and about your team."

## One-liner variants by venue

- **Show HN:** "ECHO — a local-first context layer that makes every AI tool remember you, and lets your team share only the decisions you approve."
- **Demo day:** "Every AI tool your company uses meets your people as strangers. ECHO is the context layer that fixes that — per person first, then per team, with the same privacy gate at every level."
- **First team sale (2–5 person startup):** "Your engineers get an AI memory that works day one, solo. You get answers to 'why is this the priority' without a meeting. Nothing raw ever leaves anyone's laptop."
