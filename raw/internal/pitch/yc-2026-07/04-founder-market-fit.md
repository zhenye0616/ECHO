# Founder-market fit

**Claim shape:** not "I'm passionate about AI" — *this founder has earned secrets about this specific market that can only be earned by operating at the frontier of multi-agent, multi-vendor AI work.*

## Earned secrets (from the build, all verifiable in the repo)

1. **The brain lives in the consumer, never in the substrate.** ECHO is deliberately LLM-free at the core — deterministic capture + retrieval; intelligence is supplied by whichever agent queries it. Discovered the hard way (6/19 live failure), now a load-bearing architecture principle. Most competitors bolt an LLM into the memory layer and inherit confabulation.
2. **The unit of context is behavioral sequences, not documents.** Temporal traces of what a person actually did across tools — not files, not embeddings-over-notes. This is why capture-side beats index-side.
3. **Human-gated sharing is the product, not a compliance feature.** The propose-confirm gate is simultaneously the privacy story and the alignment story — raw stays local, only approved decision-grade context flows. No competitor in the category (Mem0, Letta, Glean) has this as their primitive.
4. **Cross-vendor is structural, not aspirational.** Anthropic can't be vendor-neutral — they ARE a vendor (`2026-05-13-echo-skills-are-the-cross-tool-protocol.md`). A context layer must be owned by the user, hosted by no model vendor. The founder runs Claude AND Codex AND Cursor as peers daily through his own protocol — approximately nobody else operates this way yet.
5. **The failure taxonomy.** ~358 logged incidents reduced to six broken boundary contracts (`backlog/_followups.md`) — a depth of understanding of why cross-tool context *actually* fails that no one doing user interviews from the outside has.

## Market-position facts (from `05-tam.md` research)

The open flank all of them leave: local-first raw capture + cross-vendor serve-back + human-gated team sharing, for the AI-native small team.
